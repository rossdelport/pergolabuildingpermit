Attached vs freestanding patio

Short answer

An attached patio always needs a building permit. A freestanding one can skip it only if it's 10 m² or less, no more than 2.4 m high, and not in wind region C or D. A typical backyard patio is bigger than that, so it needs a permit either way.

Last updated 11 November 2026

Why attachment matters

Whether your patio is fixed to the house is the biggest single thing that decides if it needs a building permit. The exemption for small structures only covers freestanding ones.

The 10 m² exemption

WA's building rules let you skip a permit for a small Class 10a structure only if it's freestanding, no more than 10 m² in floor area, no more than 2.4 m high, and not in wind region C or D. The City of Wanneroo applies it to patios and says every attached patio needs a building permit, whatever its size.

A typical backyard patio is bigger than 10 m², so it needs a permit either way. The exemption is mainly for small garden pergolas and gazebos. Wanneroo counts gazebos as patios too.

Distances from the boundary and house

The building code also sets fire separation distances. Wanneroo's sheet says the roof must be 900 mm from a side or rear boundary, or 900 mm from the house if the patio is detached. The City of Cockburn says an attached patio must be 900 mm from a boundary, except one facing a road or public space. Joondalup's louvre roof guidance follows the same 900 mm rule.

Planning rules are separate. In Wanneroo, posts can sit on the boundary if the patio is no more than 10 m long and 2.7 m high, with the roof at least 500 mm back.

Which should I choose?

  • Attached: easier to keep dry between house and patio, but always needs a permit and connection details
  • Freestanding: can avoid a permit if it's very small; a larger one still needs a permit

Councils vary, so check yours. Try the patio approval checker, read about patios on the boundary, or see how to draw patio plans for council.

Questions

Common questions

Does an attached patio need a permit in WA?

Yes. The City of Wanneroo says every attached patio needs a building permit, whatever its size, because the small-structure exemption only covers freestanding ones.

Does a freestanding patio need a permit?

Not if it's 10 m² or less, no more than 2.4 m high, and not in wind region C or D. A freestanding patio bigger than that needs a permit.

How far must an attached patio be from the boundary?

The building code generally needs the roof 900 mm from a side or rear boundary, as Wanneroo and Cockburn explain. Posts can be closer under the planning rules in some cases.

Is an attached patio harder to get approved?

Not much. You need the same plans, plus details showing how it connects to the house.
