Do I need one?

Do I need council approval for a pergola in WA?

Short answer

If your pergola has a roof covering and is attached to the house, yes, it needs a building permit. Freestanding roofed pergolas are exempt only if they are 10 m² or less and 2.4 m tall or less. Open-beam pergolas are often exempt up to a size your council sets.

Last updated 29 October 2026

Two questions decide it

For a pergola in WA, the answer comes down to two things: does it have a roof covering, and is it attached to the house?

What counts as a roof covering?

Metal roof sheets, polycarbonate, glass, shade cloth fixed as a roof, and opening or adjustable louvres all count. A pergola with only open timber or steel beams does not. If you plan to add a roof later, the pergola will need a permit then, so it is easier to get it at the start.

Why "attached" matters

A pergola fixed to the house puts load on the house's walls or roof. The permit checks that the connection, the posts and the footings can take it, including wind uplift. That is why attached patios need a permit at any size.

Planning rules still apply

Even when no building permit is needed, a pergola has to follow the planning rules:

  • Front setback. Pergolas and patios in front of the house usually need planning approval.
  • Boundaries. Structures close to a side or rear boundary have setback and fire-separation rules.
  • Heritage areas and estates may have their own design rules.
  • Bushfire-prone areas can add construction requirements for structures near the house.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need council approval for a freestanding pergola?

If it has open beams only, often not, up to a size limit your council sets. If it has a roof covering, it is exempt when it is 10 m² or less and no taller than 2.4 m. Bigger roofed pergolas need a permit.

Is a pergola with shade cloth exempt?

Shade cloth fixed as a permanent roof is usually treated as a roof covering. Removable shade cloth or a small shade sail is often treated differently. Ask your council or us before you build.

What happens if I build a pergola without approval?

Your council can issue a notice to fix or remove it, and fines in WA can reach $50,000 for a first offence. It can also hold up a sale. If it is already built, retrospective approval is usually possible.
