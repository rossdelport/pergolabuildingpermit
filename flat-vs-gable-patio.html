Flat vs gable patio

Short answer

A flat patio is cheaper and lower. A gable patio costs about 10 to 15% more for a kit but gives better airflow, more light and a taller feel. Both usually need a building permit. Near a boundary, a flat roof is often easier to get approved because it's lower.

Last updated 18 November 2026

Approval differences

Both styles usually need a building permit, because the only exemption is for a freestanding structure up to 10 m² and 2.4 m high. The roof style matters most near a boundary. A gable's taller ridge and end walls can push you over the height limits councils use for boundary structures, so a flat roof is often easier on a side boundary. Councils vary, so check your height limits before you order.

Questions

Common questions

Is a flat or gable patio cheaper?

Flat is cheaper. Published Perth kit prices show gable kits costing about 10 to 15% more than flat kits of the same size.

Is a gable patio cooler than a flat patio?

Usually yes. The higher peak lets hot air rise and escape, and gives more light and airflow.

Do I need council approval for a gable patio?

Usually yes, the same as a flat patio. Only freestanding structures up to 10 m² and 2.4 m high are exempt.

Which roof is better near a boundary?

A flat roof is often easier, because a gable's taller ridge can go over the height limits councils use for boundary structures.
