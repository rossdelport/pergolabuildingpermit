Patios

Patio building permits in WA

Short answer

Yes, a patio attached to the house needs a building permit in WA, at any size. A freestanding patio is exempt only if it is 10 m² or less and 2.4 m tall or less. Patios close to a boundary can also need planning approval.

Last updated 29 October 2026

Attached patios need a permit

A patio is a roofed structure, usually open on at least two sides, used as an outdoor living area. When it is attached to the house, it needs a building permit in WA whatever its size. A freestanding patio only avoids a permit if it is 10 m² or less and 2.4 m tall or less.

Verandahs and alfresco roofs

A verandah is a roofed area along the side of the house, and it is treated much like an attached patio: it needs a building permit. Extending the main roof of the house over an alfresco area is building work to the house itself, and also needs a permit.

Enclosing a patio

Adding walls, windows or doors to a patio changes what it is. Depending on how enclosed it is, it can become a sunroom or a habitable room, which has extra rules for insulation, light, ventilation and energy efficiency. It needs a building permit, and sometimes planning approval. Talk to us before you enclose an approved patio, because the original permit won't cover it.

What a patio permit application includes

  • Site plan showing the patio, the house and the distance to each boundary
  • Elevations showing heights and the roof pitch
  • Engineering for the frame, footings and connection to the house
  • The application form, owner consent and fees

Patios near the boundary

The R-Codes allow patios fairly close to side and rear boundaries, but there are limits, and posts or roofs very close to the boundary can trigger fire-separation rules. If your patio doesn't meet the standard rules, it needs planning approval, and your neighbour may be asked for comments.

Common questions

Does a patio kit come with engineering?

Most reputable patio suppliers in Perth provide engineering certification for their kits. We use it in your application. If yours doesn't, we can arrange an engineer.

Can my patio builder lodge the permit?

Some do. Many leave it to the homeowner, or charge extra. We can handle it for you or work alongside your builder.
