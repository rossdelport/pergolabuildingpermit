Question answer

Can a patio be built on the boundary?

Short answer

Sometimes, but it's tricky. Some councils let the posts go on the boundary for small patios (Wanneroo: up to 10 m long and 2.7 m high, roof 500 mm back). But the building code usually needs the roof 900 mm from the boundary for fire safety, unless extra fire protection is designed in. Councils vary.

Last updated 8 November 2026

Two separate tests

Building a patio close to the fence has to pass two different sets of rules:

  1. Planning rules (the R-Codes and council policies) decide how close the posts and roof can be before you need planning approval.
  2. The building code (fire separation) decides how close the roof can be to the boundary for the building permit.

A patio can pass one and fail the other, so check both.

Councils vary, and your R-Code and any local development plan can change the numbers.

Why 900 mm keeps coming up

The 900 mm rule is about fire. It stops a fire spreading from your patio and house to the neighbour's place. Where the patio is too close, the building code usually needs extra fire protection, which a building surveyor or engineer would design. Ask before you assume a boundary patio is impossible.

Don't fix it to the fence

Whatever the setback, the patio should stand on its own posts and footings on your land. Councils such as Joondalup say structures shouldn't be fixed to a dividing fence or hang over the neighbour's property. If any part would go over the boundary, you need the neighbour's written consent (form BA20) before a permit can be issued.

Planning approval if you don't meet the rules

If your patio is closer than the standard setback, longer, or taller, you can still apply for planning approval. The council may ask your neighbours for comment.

Questions

Common questions

Can a patio be built on the boundary in WA?

Sometimes. Wanneroo allows posts on the boundary with the roof 500 mm back for patios up to 10 m long and 2.7 m high behind the street setback. But the building code usually wants the roof 900 mm from the boundary for fire separation. Councils vary.

How close to the fence can I build a patio in Perth?

Joondalup allows 450 mm for patios up to 2.7 m high and 10 m long, with 900 mm for attached patios under the building code. Wanneroo allows posts on the boundary in some cases. Check your council.

Why do patios need to be 900 mm from the boundary?

For fire separation under the building code. It reduces the risk of fire spreading between properties. Closer patios usually need extra fire protection.

Can I attach my patio to the dividing fence?

No. Patios should stand on their own posts on your land. Councils like Joondalup say structures shouldn't be fixed to dividing fences.

Do I need my neighbour's permission for a patio on the boundary?

Not if it's fully on your land and meets the rules. If any part goes over the boundary, you need their written consent (form BA20). The council may also ask neighbours for comment on a planning application.
