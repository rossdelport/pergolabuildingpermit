How to draw patio plans for council

Short answer

Councils usually want four things: a site plan at 1:200 showing setbacks, elevations at 1:100 showing heights, construction details, and a structural engineer's design. Wanneroo and Belmont both ask for this set with a BA2 application. Councils vary, so check yours.

Last updated 10 November 2026

The four drawings councils ask for

If you apply yourself with an uncertified application (form BA2), the council checks your drawings. Wanneroo and Belmont both ask for the same core set:

DrawingScaleWhat it shows
Site plan1:200 or largerWhole block, boundaries, house, patio and the distances between them
Elevations (and sections)1:100Side views of the patio with heights and roof pitch
Construction detailsTo suitPost, beam and roof sizes, footings, and how it connects to the house
Engineer's designTo suitA structural engineer's certification of the patio

Step 1: the site plan

Start with a copy of your lot plan or survey. Then draw on it:

  • All boundaries, with their lengths
  • The house and any other buildings, sheds or retaining walls
  • The patio, with its size and its distance to each nearby boundary and building
  • Ground levels (spot levels) and the finished floor level, as Wanneroo asks
  • Any easements and septic system
  • A north point

Measure setbacks to the posts and to the roof edge. Councils check both. In Wanneroo, for example, posts can sit on the boundary only if the patio is no more than 10 m long and 2.7 m high, with the roof at least 500 mm back.

Step 2: elevations

Draw the patio from the front and side at 1:100. Show the height at the gutter, the highest point of the roof, the roof pitch and the ground line. If the patio attaches to the house, show where and how.

Step 3: construction details and engineering

Ask your patio supplier or builder for engineering that suits your wind rating. It should cover post sizes, beam spans, footing sizes and tie-downs. If the patio is attached to the house, Wanneroo also asks for connection details.

Step 4: check before you lodge

  • Every drawing has the address, a scale and a date
  • Dimensions match across all drawings
  • Setbacks meet your council's rules, or you've applied for planning approval
  • Your council's extra forms are included. Wanneroo needs a Street and Verge Bond Permit application with every building permit.

Councils vary, so check your council's checklist. If you use a private building surveyor (form BA1), they check the drawings first.

Questions

Common questions

What plans do I need for a patio permit in WA?

Usually a site plan at 1:200, elevations at 1:100, construction details and a structural engineer's design. Councils such as Wanneroo and Belmont ask for this set with an uncertified (BA2) application.

Can I draw my own patio plans?

Usually yes. Councils ask for clear, scaled, dimensioned drawings. The engineering must come from a structural engineer, which some patio suppliers provide.

What scale should a patio site plan be?

At least 1:200 for the site plan and 1:100 for elevations, in Wanneroo and Belmont.

What should a patio site plan show?

Boundaries, existing buildings, the patio with its size and setbacks to posts and roof, ground levels, easements, any septic system and a north point. Councils vary, so check your council's checklist.
